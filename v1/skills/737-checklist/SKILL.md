# Boeing 737 checklist execution

1. Use `list_checklist_categories` and `read_checklist` to obtain the live category, sections, item identifiers, conditions, and expected responses. Read each section when its procedure becomes applicable. Use the tools' current schema to establish the active section if required; do not assume section transitions are automatic.
2. Use the default authenticated context unless the tool requires an explicitly supplied identifier. Never invent user or run identifiers. On an access failure, retry with the supported default context; if still blocked, report the issue and continue the physical procedure safely without claiming recorded completion.
3. For each item: establish its stated condition, perform or verify the action, read the relevant aircraft indications, then call `mark_checklist` with the live identifiers and required response. Reading a procedure or changing a switch does not record completion.
4. Verify that the mark is accepted and that checklist progress changes. Acceptance records your assertion; it does not supply missing aircraft evidence. Before leaving a section, read it back and resolve unmarked applicable items. For a conditional branch that does not apply, verify the actual configuration and use documented not-applicable recording if available; otherwise report the accounting limitation, not a completed action. Never select an unnecessary warning inhibit to satisfy an inapplicable item. Do not mark future, unverified, or failed actions complete; a historical trigger must have been observed, not inferred from the present state.
5. Prepare the upcoming airborne items while stopped. During rotation or recovery, aircraft control takes priority; retain the verified evidence and record it at the next safe opportunity without leaving a sustained control input unattended.

## Annunciator recall and arrival closeout

- At initialization and before descent/approach, read `fire-and-emergency` with `read_group` and inspect the relevant system indications. Routine flight-deck instrument reads do not include all warnings and cautions.
- For a system-annunciator Recall item, check the relevant control documentation and use the documented recall control, then read the recalled annunciators and affected systems before marking it. Resetting a master-caution light or reading clear annunciators is not a recall. If the control is unavailable or recall cannot be verified, leave the item unmarked, report the limitation once, and continue the safe procedure; do not search unrelated cockpit groups while approaching an intercept.
- Identify each active warning or caution and follow its documented procedure while maintaining aircraft control. Acknowledge/reset a latched alert only after identifying the cause and confirming the appropriate response, then read both master indications and the underlying system annunciators again. Do not repeatedly reset, inhibit alerts, or change required configuration merely to extinguish lights.
- An ICE system annunciator points to the ice-protection systems, not necessarily atmospheric icing. Read `anti-ice-and-wipers`, including probe-heat selections, captain/first-officer PITOT OFF indications, window heat, and engine/wing anti-ice states. Cold temperature and dewpoint alone do not diagnose the caution or justify turning on engine/wing anti-ice; resolve any icing decision separately under the applicable procedure.
- After stopping and completing the applicable after-landing configuration, including any prescribed probe-heat OFF selection, repeat the warning/caution and system read-back: cleanup can introduce new indications. PITOT OFF indications can be expected with probe heat intentionally OFF on the ground. If system evidence establishes that this expected configuration is the sole cause of an ICE/master caution and other systems are normal, acknowledge it once with the documented master-caution reset control, then reread the master indication, system annunciators, and probe-heat/PITOT indications. Keep the required probe-heat configuration; acknowledgement is not a repair or a Recall, and expected PITOT OFF indications need not disappear. Report any recurring, unexplained, or unreadable caution rather than repeatedly resetting it or claiming all warnings clear.

## Takeoff conditions to preserve

- Before runway entry, verify actual runway identity and runway-entry authority. In a runway-loaded scenario, verify the initial runway and the scenario's stated authority rather than inventing a prior clearance.
- At entry, verify the required strobe/other lights and both transponder code and mode. After takeoff authority, verify the required fixed landing lights and approximately 40% N1 on both engines.
- After engine stabilization, verify TO/GA outcome; at the applicable VR, verify rotation toward 15°; at positive climb, verify gear retraction.
- Above the procedure's stated 400 ft datum, verify the permitted roll mode and scheduled flap retraction. Height alone does not establish safe flap-retraction speed.
- Only after actual flap/slat retraction, complete the prescribed VNAV selection, autopilot decision, engine start-switch positions, autobrake OFF, and gear-lever OFF checks. Verify actual modes and configuration, not just switch commands.
- For gear-lever OFF, retracted gear and a handle-not-down indication do not distinguish UP from OFF. Require evidence that distinguishes the OFF detent. If the selection remains unverified and no such indication is available, leave that item unmarked and report the limitation while continuing safe flight; reporting it does not justify marking it complete.
